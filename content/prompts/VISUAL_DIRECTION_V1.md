# VISUAL_DIRECTION_V1

## Position

ForgeView is a public AI trading research lab, not a signal channel.

The visual identity should communicate:

- evidence before confidence;
- probability before prediction;
- executable prices before simulated profit;
- failed hypotheses as useful research;
- systems engineering as part of market research.

The Probability Lab repository is the primary source. Do not invent trades,
returns, wallet behavior, sample sizes, or model performance.

## Visual Thesis

**A cinematic quantitative research terminal built in public.**

The viewer should feel that they have entered a dark institutional research
room where a live market hypothesis is being tested, challenged, and either
advanced or rejected.

This is not crypto neon, retail trading hype, luxury imagery, or generic AI
glow. It is restrained, technical, legible, and evidence-led.

## Art Direction

### Environment

- Dark charcoal research room, not pure black.
- Dense but controlled terminal surfaces.
- Real charts, order-book ladders, code, timestamps, and experiment labels.
- Subtle depth from glass reflections, monitor light, and shallow focus.
- No fake trading floors, sports cars, coins, rockets, or anonymous hoodies.

### Palette

- Background: `#080B10`, `#0D1219`, `#131A23`
- Primary text: `#F2F5F7`
- Secondary text: `#8D99A8`
- Data cyan: `#37C7F4`
- Confirmed green: `#38D996`
- Warning amber: `#F4B942`
- Rejected red: `#F05B61`
- Probability violet: `#8A7CFF`, used sparingly

Green never means "buy." It means confirmed, complete, or passed.
Red never means "sell." It means rejected, failed, or invalid.

### Typography

- Condensed grotesk for hooks and verdicts.
- Monospaced font for data, code, market IDs, timestamps, and metrics.
- Maximum two font families.
- Short labels, large numerals, strong hierarchy.
- No oversized paragraph cards.

### Graphic Language

- Probability heatmaps.
- Calibration curves.
- Brier score comparisons.
- Timeline rulers: 4m, 3m, 2m, 1m, 30s.
- Order-book bid/ask ladders.
- Market-resolution countdowns.
- Wallet-entry synchronization plots.
- Equity curves and drawdown strips.
- Dataset counters and sample-size warnings.
- Code diffs, retry logs, and `MARKET_SWITCH` events.

## Motion System

- 25-40 second runtime.
- 6-7 scenes.
- One visual claim per scene.
- Camera motion is slow and deliberate; data motion is fast and precise.
- Use hard cuts for hypothesis changes.
- Use scan lines or cursor movement only when tied to actual data.
- Charts should animate from raw points to conclusion.
- Show uncertainty through ranges, confidence bands, missing-data gaps, and
  sample counters.
- Final verdict lands as a stamped research status:
  `SUPPORTED`, `REJECTED`, `NOT CONFIRMED`, or `COLLECTING DATA`.

## Recurring Shot Types

1. **Cold open:** one contradiction in large type over a live terminal.
2. **Evidence wall:** two or three synchronized charts reveal a pattern.
3. **Microscope:** zoom into one trade, timing bucket, or ask price.
4. **Simulation room:** particles or paths represent alternate outcomes.
5. **Code intervention:** show the exact recorder/model logic that changed.
6. **Reality check:** replace simulated price with captured executable ask.
7. **Verdict frame:** state status, sample size, and next falsification test.

## Content-to-Visual Mapping

| Research source | Primary visual |
| --- | --- |
| Wallet overlap | synchronized event timeline |
| Both-outcome buying | split YES/NO order ladder |
| Entry timing | countdown histogram |
| Calibration | predicted-vs-actual curve |
| Model comparison | Brier/log-loss score board |
| Simulated PnL | model-price vs execution-price waterfall |
| Failed hypothesis | red hypothesis card collapsing into evidence |
| Recorder bug | frozen market ID and stalled countdown |
| Timeout resilience | retry/backoff log with recovered data stream |
| Real validation | captured ask, resolution, payout, and sample counter |

## Editorial Rules

- Every Short names the research status.
- Every performance number includes its sample context.
- Simulated and real executable results must look visually different.
- Use `SIMULATION` watermark on modeled PnL scenes.
- Use `REAL CAPTURED ASK` label only when sourced from recorder data.
- Never display a trade instruction, position size, entry call, or wallet CTA.
- Never imply that predictive edge equals profitable edge.
- The strongest recurring line is: **"The model was right. Was the price
  executable?"**
